Quantum Computing Principles

Quantum superposition is a fundamental principle of quantum mechanics. It states that, much like waves in classical physics, any two (or more) quantum states can be added together ("superposed") and the result will be another valid quantum state.

Mathematical Formulation

In quantum mechanics, a state vector |ψ⟩ in a complex Hilbert space represents the state of a quantum system.

|ψ⟩ = α|0⟩ + β|1⟩

Where |α|² + |β|² = 1

The coefficients α and β are probability amplitudes.

The normalization condition |α|² + |β|² = 1 ensures that the total probability of finding the system in either state |0⟩ or |1⟩ is exactly 1 (or 100%). It is a conservation of probability intrinsic to unitary evolution in quantum mechanics.
