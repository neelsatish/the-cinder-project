# Cinder host AI: feasibility, hardware, and validation plan

Research date: 19 October 2026. Status: planning research; no model was installed or benchmarked and no application code was changed. This report uses live web research because an installed deep research skill was not found. Hardware prices are seller-page snapshots, not quotations or seller endorsements.

## Decision

Prototype **Qwen3.5-4B at Q4_K_M on a shared host with a used RTX 3060 12GB**, a six-core CPU, 16GB minimum / 32GB preferred system RAM, and a 500GB minimum / 1TB preferred SSD. Reuse a suitable tower before buying an entire computer. Benchmark a 2B model and Ministral 3 3B alongside it before selecting the final model.

This is the cheapest sensible configuration to investigate for the requested mixed workload. It is **not a demonstrated minimum that guarantees ten simultaneous answers within 30 seconds**. Eight-gigabyte cards can run restricted configurations and deserve a cheaper pilot if already available. A new 16GB GPU is an upgrade option after measuring a bottleneck, not a prerequisite for starting.

Planning allowances, excluding monitor, peripherals, UPS, development, and any external search/API service:

| Purchase route | Allowance | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---|
| Existing suitable tower with adequate RAM/SSD; add a used 12GB GPU and possibly PSU | ₹30,000–₹45,000 additional | Cheapest starting route; verify slot, power connectors, clearance, and cooling |
| Mixed refurbished tower and used 12GB GPU, with required SSD/PSU/RAM work | ₹55,000–₹85,000 total | Sourcing target, not a fully quoted compatible bill of materials |
| Mostly new desktop platform, 32GB RAM, used 12GB GPU | ₹75,000–₹1,00,000 total | Higher allowance reflects current RAM/SSD pricing uncertainty |
| New platform and new 16GB NVIDIA GPU | Roughly ₹1,35,000–₹1,70,000 | Not the low-cost starting recommendation; still requires throughput validation |

If the school already owns a compatible GPU computer, the initial hardware spend can be zero. Conversely, nothing in this research establishes an inexpensive purchase that meets the complete 5–10-request latency requirement; the pilot is the purchasing gate.

## Requirements agreed in this chat

- Minimize cost; identify hardware rather than assume a budget or existing machine.
- Serve 5–10 active users, aiming to complete ordinary answers in under 30 seconds.
- Work on spreadsheets, math/science, and past examination papers.
- Include CBSE, ICSE/ISC, Cambridge IGCSE, and AS/A Level, with lower school material where applicable.
- Accept digital PDFs, scans, handwriting, and diagrams.
- Work offline using uploaded question papers and marking schemes, and online using matching retrieved sources.
- Suggest marks and show proposed changes for teacher review. Do not automatically publish grades.

Assumptions for sizing: English is the first benchmark language because languages were not specified; language coverage remains a deployment requirement to confirm. “Under 30 seconds” means completed ordinary responses, not merely first-token streaming. Full papers are measured separately and are not silently excluded from the overall feasibility assessment.

## Models to test

| Model | Parameter interpretation | Proposed role | Material limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Qwen3.5-4B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B) | Model card specifies a 4B language model with a vision encoder | First overall candidate for text, images, and tool-assisted work | Thinking can generate substantial additional tokens; benchmark handwriting and rubric compliance directly |
| [Qwen3.5-2B](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-2B) | 2B-class language model, with vision overhead | Lower-memory and latency baseline | Do not infer marking quality from its size or from OCR benchmarks |
| [Ministral 3 3B Instruct](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Ministral-3-3B-Instruct-2512) | 3.4B language model + 0.4B vision encoder | Alternative with native function calling and JSON output | Different language coverage and architecture; compare on identical Cinder tasks |

These model cards list Apache 2.0 licensing. Preserve applicable notices and check the exact downloaded artifact and accompanying files before distribution. Loading these open-weight models locally does not require a per-token model-provider charge; operation, maintenance, and optional online services still have costs.

Do not confuse effective or active parameters with total stored parameters. Google's [Gemma 4 model card](https://ai.google.dev/gemma/docs/core/model_card_4) lists E2B as 2.3B effective but 5.1B including embeddings, and E4B as 4.5B effective but 8B including embeddings, plus modality encoders. They are useful optional comparisons if measured footprint wins, but they are not literal 2B/4B-total models. Likewise, an A3B/A4B mixture-of-experts model can store many more parameters than it activates per token.

I would not begin with fine-tuning. First retrieve the exact question and marking-scheme excerpt into the prompt. This keeps new sessions, board variants, and teacher corrections inspectable without training a new model.

## Storage and runtime memory

Quantization reduces numerical precision and storage; it does not turn a 4B model into a smaller parameter-count model. File sizes are decimal GB and runtime memory is additional.

| Artifact | Language GGUF | Separate vision file | Combined download, approximately |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Qwen3.5-2B Q4_K_M | 1.28 GB | F16 projector 0.668 GB | 1.95 GB |
| Qwen3.5-4B Q4_K_M | 2.74 GB | F16 projector 0.672 GB | 3.41 GB |
| Ministral 3 3B Q4_K_M | 2.15 GB | BF16 projector 0.842 GB | 2.99 GB |

Sources: [Qwen 2B quantizer files](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Qwen3.5-2B-GGUF/tree/main), [Qwen 4B quantizer files](https://huggingface.co/unsloth/Qwen3.5-4B-GGUF/tree/main), and [Mistral's own GGUF files](https://huggingface.co/mistralai/Ministral-3-3B-Instruct-2512-GGUF/tree/main). Obtain a compatible model/projector pair and record revisions and checksums. Download one chosen quantization, not the entire multi-quantization repository.

Additional GPU memory is needed for attention cache, recurrent state, image processing, temporary buffers, and the inference runtime. Shared model weights generally need not be duplicated for every user, but each concurrent sequence needs state.

An analytical illustration, not an observed allocation: the [Qwen 4B configuration](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B/raw/main/config.json) has eight full-attention layers, four KV heads, and head dimension 256. With 16-bit K and V, the full-attention cache component is 8 × 2 × 4 × 256 × 2 = 32,768 bytes/token. At 8,192 tokens per sequence that component alone is 0.25 GiB, or 2.5 GiB for ten sequences; at 16,384 tokens it becomes 5 GiB for ten. Recurrent states, checkpoints, weights, buffers, and image work are excluded. Actual engines can allocate differently or quantize caches.

Consequently, an 8GB GPU may fit one or several short jobs, 12GB is a reasonable shared-host pilot, and 16GB offers more headroom. None of those capacities establishes response speed. Start the benchmark at 8K and 16K per-request context and retrieve small relevant excerpts. Test whether this truncation changes quality: [Qwen's card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B) recommends much longer context for its complex thinking workloads, so short-context deployment must not inherit its published benchmark claims.

## What the 30-second target requires

For ten simultaneous users each receiving 300 generated tokens, the server must produce 3,000 tokens. That requires at least 100 aggregate output tokens/second even if the entire 30 seconds is available for generation. Allowing ten seconds for queuing, prompt/image processing, and tools increases the output requirement to 150 aggregate tokens/second. Hidden thinking and repeated tool-call turns count too. Five simultaneous requests halve these generation-only requirements.

One author's [RTX 4060 Laptop 8GB experiment](https://www.insidentally.com/articles/000051/) reports approximately 69 generated tokens/second on GPU versus 10 on CPU in a short Qwen3.5-4B llama-bench run. This is primary evidence from that tester, not a controlled independent reproduction, an RTX 3060 measurement, or a handwriting/concurrency result. It supports investigating a GPU; it does not prove Cinder's service target.

The [llama.cpp server](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/blob/master/tools/server/README.md) supports concurrent sequences, continuous batching, multimodal requests, and structured JSON. Batching can improve total throughput, but per-user latency must be measured. Do not multiply a single-user speed by ten, divide by ten as a precise prediction, or mistake ten connected clients for ten simultaneous inferences.

Proposed operating policy to benchmark:

- Short spreadsheet commands, retrieved explanations, and individual-answer feedback use the interactive queue.
- Scanned full papers use a persistent background queue, with page/question progress and resumable results.
- Test 2 and 4 executing sequences first, then 5 and 10 if memory and latency allow. This is a candidate tuning policy, not assumed compliance with ten simultaneous requests.
- Keep long reasoning available for difficult questions, but show that it may exceed the interactive target.
- Report waiting time honestly. Streaming a status message is not completion.

No precise full-paper completion time is established. It depends on pages, image resolution, handwriting quality, questions, reasoning length, and other users. If every entire handwritten paper must finish in 30 seconds even during a ten-user burst, this report does not establish feasibility on the proposed budget.

The CPU floor is an engineering proposal, not a model manufacturer's certified minimum. Image preprocessing, PDF extraction, background fetches, and Cinder itself share host resources. With GPU inference, CPU memory speed is less central than in CPU-only inference, but it still matters.

[NVIDIA's RTX 3060 specification](https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/geforce/graphics-cards/30-series/rtx-3060-3060ti/) lists 170W card power and a 550W reference system recommendation; exact board connectors and dimensions vary. A mini PC or small office desktop may lack room, PSU capacity, or suitable connectors. Check the actual system before assuming a cheap refurbished machine can take the GPU. Do not use a SATA power adapter to make an unsuitable PSU fit the plan.

## Online and offline workflow

Use one local model service on the host. The other machines call Cinder over the LAN; they do not each load the model. Losing internet is different from losing contact with the host: host-based AI still needs a functioning local connection.

Offline marking:

1. A teacher uploads the question paper, marking scheme, and student answers, or uses a previously prepared local paper pack.
2. Store board, level, subject/syllabus code, year, session, paper/component, variant, language, and source/version. For lower classes, a school rubric may be the relevant authority.
3. Extract digital text directly; process scans and handwritten regions as images. Preserve the original page and crop coordinates. Index the marking scheme once per paper.
4. For each question retrieve the matching question, scheme entries, and student answer crop. Flag unreadable symbols instead of silently inventing a transcription.
5. Use calculation tools for checkable arithmetic/algebra and unit rules; use the rubric to propose marks, including allowed method/follow-through credit.
6. Show suggested marks, the original answer, the cited rubric passage, and the reason for a proposed change. Ambiguous handwriting, alternative methods, and difficult diagrams remain visibly flagged for review.
7. Apply only teacher-accepted changes and retain the prior grade and audit entry.

Online mode adds discovery and download of the exact matching paper/scheme. It does not remove the need for a rubric. If the correct scheme cannot be retrieved or verified, request an upload or clearly label general practice feedback; do not represent a generic web answer as board-authorized marking.

Official starting points are [CBSE past papers](https://www.cbse.gov.in/cbsenew/question-paper.html), [CBSE marking schemes](https://www.cbse.gov.in/cbsenew/marking-scheme.html), [Cambridge subject past-paper pages](https://www.cambridgeinternational.org/programmes-and-qualifications/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580/past-papers/), and [CISCE's specimen-paper publication notice](https://www.cisce.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/05/Specimen-Question-Papers-in-selected-subjects-for-ICSE-and-ISC-Year-2025.pdf). Public availability varies by board and examination; a specimen paper is not automatically the matching past paper. Cambridge also documents resource access in its [help article](https://help.cambridgeinternational.org/hc/en-gb/articles/115004448905-Where-can-I-find-past-papers-mark-schemes-and-resources-for-our-exams).

Build structured download/index adapters for known official sources before a general browsing agent. Use ordinary HTTP for static pages/PDF links and a headless browser only when the source actually needs it. Cache verified downloads with URLs, hashes, and metadata; do not repeatedly scrape the same paper for every student. Access-controlled resources require an authorized route; an upload remains the reliable offline/import path. An optional general search provider has separate availability and possible cost.

## Spreadsheets and calculation tools

Let the model propose operations against a bounded sheet range; let the spreadsheet engine perform calculation. Validate sheet IDs, ranges, cell types, formulas, permissions, and expected old values. Present an old/new-cell diff and apply it only after acceptance, including grade changes. Keep rollback/audit information. This is compatible with the Univer-style workflow described in Cinder's project instructions, but the current implementation was not audited in this task.

For math, [SymPy supports symbolic and numeric equation solving](https://docs.sympy.org/latest/guides/solving/). It can help verify results under explicit assumptions; it cannot certify a free-form scientific explanation or automatically award all rubric marks. Use constrained tool operations rather than allowing the model arbitrary host code execution. If XLSX tooling is involved, note that [openpyxl does not evaluate formulas](https://openpyxl.readthedocs.io/en/3.1.2/simple_formulae.html); writing a formula is not evidence that its result was recalculated.

Fetched pages, PDFs, and workbook content are untrusted data. They must not grant new tool permissions. Restrict web tools from accessing local/admin network endpoints or sending student submissions to unrelated sites. Keep spreadsheet writes behind the same validated review flow even if a document contains instructions to change grades.

## Validation plan before purchasing or promising capacity

1. **Prepare a representative evaluation pack.** Start with 200 teacher-marked question/answer pairs stratified across requested boards, lower/upper grade bands, math and sciences, correct/incorrect/partial answers, scans, handwriting, and diagrams. Add 30 spreadsheet tasks, 20 exact paper/scheme retrieval cases, and ten complete scanned papers. Include unseen variants and deliberately mismatched schemes. Two teachers should adjudicate ambiguous marking cases so model disagreement is not mistaken for a uniquely wrong answer.
2. **Compare model quality first.** Run the same pack through 2B Q4, 4B Q4, and Ministral 3 3B Q4. Compare a higher-precision 4B baseline on a representative subset. Measure exact marks agreement, absolute mark error, accepted alternatives, incorrect confident transcriptions, rubric citation correctness, and teacher correction effort. Scores from model cards are not Cinder results.
3. **Measure end-to-end load.** Use the actual candidate host at 1, 5, and 10 simultaneous requests plus a realistic staggered session. Measure upload-to-completed-response time, queueing, first-token time, prompt/image processing, all generated/thinking tokens, tool time, GPU/system peak memory, and pages/minute for full papers. Run with background marking present. Record warm and cold results and repeat the workload on Windows and Linux.
4. **Use explicit proposed gates.** Interactive p95 completion at or below 30 seconds at each advertised load; no out-of-memory failures; exact expected spreadsheet changes in every acceptance case; no grade publishing without acceptance; no rubric mismatch silently accepted; all displayed evidence links resolve to the corresponding local/source material. For marking, propose at least 90% exact per-question agreement and 95% within one mark on the evaluation set, report abstention/coverage separately, and review results by board, level, and input type. These are proposed product gates, not measured achievements or guarantees of unseen correctness.
5. **Choose the cheapest passing configuration.** If 2B passes quality and is faster, select it. If 4B passes on an existing 8GB card, do not buy extra VRAM without a measured need. If memory is the bottleneck, try a 12/16GB configuration; if throughput is the bottleneck, compare a faster GPU or additional worker rather than assuming VRAM fixes speed. If difficult senior-school questions fail, retain teacher review and test a larger model or optional online escalation instead of certifying 4B for every task.

The final purchasing decision should include a compatible seller quote, measured capacity, per-board accuracy results, and a clear statement of which full-paper tasks are asynchronous. The researched recommendation is a low-cost 4B vision-model pilot with deterministic tools and local paper packs; production suitability remains an empirical question.
